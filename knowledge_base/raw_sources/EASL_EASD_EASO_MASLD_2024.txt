EASL-EASD-EASO CLINICAL PRACTICE GUIDELINES ON THE MANAGEMENT OF METABOLIC DYSFUNCTION-ASSOCIATED STEATOTIC LIVER DISEASE (MASLD)
Published by: European Association for the Study of the Liver (EASL), European Association for the Study of Diabetes (EASD), European Association for the Study of Obesity (EASO). Journal of Hepatology 2024;81(3):492-542.

SECTION 1: NOMENCLATURE AND DEFINITION
- MASLD (metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease, formerly termed non-alcoholic fatty liver disease / NAFLD) is defined as the presence of hepatic steatosis in conjunction with at least one cardiometabolic risk factor (overweight/obesity, dysglycemia, hypertension, hypertriglyceridemia, or low HDL) and the absence of harmful alcohol consumption.

SECTION 4.1: LIFESTYLE INTERVENTIONS & DIETARY RECOMMENDATIONS
- Lifestyle modification encompassing dietary changes, physical activity, and weight loss is the fundamental first-line therapy for all patients with MASLD.
- Weight Loss Targets:
  1. A weight loss of at least 5% of body weight is recommended to reduce hepatic steatosis and improve hepatic insulin resistance (Level of evidence 1, Grade: Strong).
  2. A weight loss of 7% to 10% is recommended to induce resolution of steatohepatitis (MASH) and stabilize or reverse hepatic fibrosis (Level of evidence 1, Grade: Strong).
- Dietary Pattern:
  1. The Mediterranean dietary pattern is recommended for all patients with MASLD to reduce liver fat and decrease cardiovascular risk (Level of evidence 1, Grade: Strong).
  2. The Mediterranean diet emphasizes extra virgin olive oil, vegetables, legumes, whole grains, nuts, fruits, and moderate fish intake, while minimizing red meat, saturated fats, and ultra-processed foods.
  3. Avoid sugar-sweetened beverages and foods high in added fructose, as fructose specifically stimulates de novo lipogenesis in the liver and accelerates steatosis.
  4. Alcohol consumption should be strictly minimized or avoided entirely in patients with MASLD.

SECTION 4.2: PHYSICAL ACTIVITY AND EXERCISE
- Both aerobic exercise and resistance training reduce hepatic steatosis even in the absence of significant weight loss.
- Patients with MASLD should aim for 150 to 300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic physical activity per week, complemented by resistance training 2 to 3 days per week.
