CARBOHYDRATE INTAKE FOR ADULTS AND CHILDREN: WHO GUIDELINE (2023)
Published by: World Health Organization (Geneva), ISBN 978-92-4-007359-3.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & RECOMMENDATIONS ON DIETARY FIBRE AND CARBOHYDRATE QUALITY:
Recommendation 1: Carbohydrate Sources & Quality
- WHO recommends that dietary carbohydrate intake should come primarily from whole grains, vegetables, fruits and pulses (Strong recommendation, moderate-certainty evidence).
- Higher intake of whole grains, vegetables, fruits, and legumes is consistently associated with significantly lower risk of all-cause mortality, type 2 diabetes, cardiovascular disease, coronary heart disease, and colorectal cancer.

Recommendation 2: Quantitative Dietary Fibre Targets for Adults
- WHO recommends that adults consume at least 25 g per day of naturally occurring dietary fibre in foods (Strong recommendation, moderate-certainty evidence).
- Naturally occurring dietary fibre refers to non-digestible carbohydrates and lignin that are intrinsic and intact in plants.
- Consuming at least 25 g/day of dietary fibre provides substantial protective effects on glycemic regulation, fasting glucose, insulin sensitivity, lipid clearance (lowering LDL and triglycerides), and normal stool physiology.

Mechanism & Gut Health Rationale:
- Dietary fibre serves as non-digestible prebiotic substrate fermented by colonic microbiota into beneficial short-chain fatty acids (SCFAs, including acetate, propionate, and butyrate).
- Increasing the consumption and variety of fiber-rich plant foods promotes microbial diversity and supports gut barrier integrity.
- Dietary recommendations must emphasize diverse whole foods (oats, barley, lentils, chickpeas, beans, flaxseed, berries, leafy vegetables) rather than isolated fiber supplements alone.
