DIAGNOSIS AND MANAGEMENT OF THE METABOLIC SYNDROME: AHA/NHLBI SCIENTIFIC STATEMENT (2005)
Published by: American Heart Association & National Heart, Lung, and Blood Institute, Grundy SM, Cleeman JI, Daniels SR, et al. Circulation 2005;112:2735-2752.
RESTRICTION NOTICE: Restricted strictly to diagnostic criteria explanation and biomarker context. Strictly prohibited from serving as primary authority for modern lifestyle recommendations or drug therapy.

CRITERIA FOR CLINICAL DIAGNOSIS OF THE METABOLIC SYNDROME:
Metabolic syndrome is clinically identified when a patient presents with 3 or more of the following 5 risk factors:
1. Elevated Waist Circumference (Abdominal Adiposity):
   - Men: Waist circumference >= 102 cm (>= 40 inches).
   - Women: Waist circumference >= 88 cm (>= 35 inches).
   (Note: Lower ethnic-specific thresholds, e.g. >= 90 cm men and >= 80 cm women, apply to Asian populations).
2. Elevated Triglycerides:
   - Serum triglycerides >= 150 mg/dL (>= 1.7 mmol/L), or drug treatment for elevated triglycerides.
3. Reduced HDL Cholesterol:
   - Men: Serum HDL < 40 mg/dL (< 1.03 mmol/L).
   - Women: Serum HDL < 50 mg/dL (< 1.29 mmol/L), or drug treatment for reduced HDL.
4. Elevated Blood Pressure:
   - Systolic blood pressure >= 130 mmHg and/or Diastolic blood pressure >= 85 mmHg, or antihypertensive drug therapy in a patient with a history of hypertension.
5. Elevated Fasting Glucose:
   - Fasting blood glucose >= 100 mg/dL (>= 5.6 mmol/L), or drug treatment for elevated glucose.
